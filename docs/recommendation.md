# Recommendation for a public-report investigation workbench

Use the tool to prioritize questions for investigation. The retrospective evaluation measures review workload; it does not demonstrate defect detection accuracy.

The default growth rule produces about three model-year/component groups per monthly cutoff in this cohort, compared with about nine groups above the same volume minimum. This is a useful starting workload. An equal-budget volume baseline shares 66 of the growth rule's 133 monthly selections. Different selections require evidence review before deciding which method is more useful.

The Civic fuel-system case shows why narrative reading matters: three of six sampled reports concern recall-repair access, two describe operating symptoms, and one is insufficient. An existing campaign predates the reporting window. Investigating parts availability and repair completion could therefore be more relevant than calling the alert a newly emerging defect.

The Accord engine case shows a different limitation. Recent counts of 25 and 59 did not trigger growth flags because earlier counts were higher. Five of six sampled accounts mention head-gasket or coolant concerns, with varying detail and no independently verified common cause. A growth-only queue can omit steady-volume groups worth reading.

These are assisted exploratory readings. All 33 user-review assignments remain pending at preparation of this memo. The sample is small, purposefully selected, and unblinded. It cannot establish comparative precision or justify an optimal threshold.

## Proposed operating approach

Keep the growth queue, add a bounded volume-review queue, and review reported severe outcomes independently of these thresholds. Document how much review time each lane receives, with overlaps counted once. Treat this as a proposed workflow until reviewed evidence and stakeholder feedback support it.

Separate recall-repair access from operating symptoms in evidence notes. A campaign reference is context, not a confirmed complaint-to-defect match. Add symptom coding only after reviewing examples and agreeing on ambiguous cases. Preserve unknowns and allow multiple symptoms per account.

Receipt-time comparisons should remain the monitoring basis because a report cannot be used before receipt. Incident dates should help interpretation: the date audit found a median lag of 12 days and 742 reports delayed more than a year. Current revised records prevent exact historical replay. Model labels and campaign descriptions also do not guarantee trim-level coverage.

## Information needed from an employer

Internal repair orders, diagnostic codes, part findings, recall-completion records, dealer scheduling and parts supply would help distinguish defect hypotheses from service-access issues. Registered or sold vehicles, mileage, and build-date/variant coverage would supply denominators and scope. Public report counts alone cannot estimate failure rates or financial loss.
